Open question

Why no gain at high concurrency

Each DSpark step yields accept_len tokens at the cost of 7 sequential draft forwards + one target forward over 8 positions. At low concurrency we are memory-bandwidth bound and verifying 8 positions is nearly free; at high concurrency compute saturates and the cost is ~8×.

But by current common understanding, DSpark should not lose throughput at large batch — so this is more likely an implementation issue on AMD rather than inherent to the method. Piotr is investigating; we will also profile batch sizes on production traffic.

Comparison

DFlash vs DSpark

Same corpus, taps, batch and lr. DFlash matches at the first position but collapses in the tail — DSpark's Markov head models dependencies between adjacent positions within a block, so error does not accumulate along the block.

Loss: DFlash is pure CE; DSpark is CE 0.1 + TV 0.9 + conf 1.0, and TV is exactly the acceptance criterion.

1 · Concurrency sweep

Concurrency 1/2/4/8/16/32 to fill in the middle range and locate the break-even point. No retraining needed.

2 · block_size sweep

7 → 4/3/2. Positions 4–6 contribute only 0.40; a shorter block may cut per-step cost. No retraining needed.

3 · Bigger draft

~1 of headroom against the official curve; 3L → 5L already gave +0.33.

4 · On-policy data

Rollout accept length is set by the traffic distribution; train on the model's own drifted state (Draft-OPD).

The drafter is not the bottleneck — same-stack references are 3.65–3.99, we are at 3.707. The bottleneck is per-step cost at high concurrency.
